# Pedagogical Frameworks & Cognitive Science Principles

Applying these cognitive learning principles ensures summaries are not merely compressed text, but active instruments of human learning.

---

## 1. Bloom’s Revised Taxonomy

Educational summaries must progress from foundational recall to creative synthesis:

```
      [Create]        ── Design, construct, formulate a solution
     [Evaluate]       ── Judge, critique, justify a design or argument
     [Analyze]        ── Compare, contrast, differentiate mechanism
     [Apply]          ── Solve problems in novel contexts using the rule
    [Understand]      ── Explain ideas or concepts in own words
    [Remember]        ── Recall basic terms, definitions, formulas
```

*Application in Summaries*:
- Ground the notes in **Remember & Understand** (clear definitions and plain-English translation).
- Structure application examples around **Apply & Analyze** (case scenarios and comparison matrices).
- Pose self-check questions that reach **Evaluate & Create** (e.g., "What happens if constraint X is violated?").

---

## 2. Cognitive Load Theory (Sweller)

Human working memory has limited capacity (4±1 novel chunks). High cognitive load impairs retention:

- **Intrinsic Load**: The inherent difficulty of the subject matter.
  *Strategy*: Deconstruct complex topics into modular sub-concepts with clear prerequisites.
- **Extraneous Load**: The mental effort wasted on parsing poor structure, verbose text, or messy formatting.
  *Strategy*: Eliminate fluff, use tables for comparisons, format mathematical formulas cleanly, and bold anchor keywords.
- **Germane Load**: The mental effort directed toward constructing schemas and long-term mental models.
  *Strategy*: Use relatable real-world analogies, flowcharts, and self-test prompts.

---

## 3. The Feynman Technique

Richard Feynman’s method for testing true comprehension:
1. **Choose the concept**.
2. **Teach it to a 12-year-old**: Use everyday vocabulary and tangible analogies. If you rely on complex jargon, you haven't mastered the core idea.
3. **Identify knowledge gaps**: Spot where your simple explanation stalls or becomes vague.
4. **Return to the source material to refine and simplify**.

*Summary implementation*:
Always pair every formal academic definition with an *Intuitive Analogy* section.

---

## 4. Active Recall & Spaced Repetition (The Testing Effect)

Passive reading leads to the "illusion of competence" (students recognize material and mistakenly believe they have learned it). True learning occurs through the cognitive struggle of retrieval.

*Summary implementation*:
Every educational summary generated MUST include a **Knowledge Check** section with questions separated from answers so students can practice retrieval before checking the key.
